Council approves library renovation after long debate

By Tom Okafor, local government reporter · 14 March 2026

Riverside's city council voted 6 to 3 on Tuesday night to approve the long-delayed renovation of the Elm Street library, ending more than two years of debate over the building's future.

The plan includes a new roof, step-free access at both entrances, a larger children's section and a community room that local groups can book for free. Construction is expected to begin in the autumn and last about fourteen months.

The total cost of the project is estimated at $4.2 million, with about half coming from a state grant awarded last year. Councillors who voted against the plan said they supported the renovation in principle but wanted more detail on how the remaining money would be raised.

During the public comment period, more than thirty residents spoke, most of them in favour. A retired teacher described the library as the only quiet, warm place in the neighbourhood where teenagers can do homework after school.

The branch will close in September, and a temporary reading room will open in the former post office on Main Street. Library cards, holds and borrowing limits will not change during the closure.

The council also heard an update on the riverside cycle path, which is on schedule to open in June.
