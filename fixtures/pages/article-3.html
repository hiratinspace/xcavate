Why I keep a paper notebook of everything I order online

By Sam Delgado · Personal finance column

Every time I order something online, I write it down: the date, the shop, what I bought and the total I paid. It takes about twenty seconds, and it has saved me more money than any budgeting app I have tried.

The habit started after I noticed a charge on my card statement that I did not recognise. It turned out to be a magazine I had signed up for months earlier and forgotten about. If I had written it down at the time, I would have remembered to stop it.

The notebook also helps with returns. When a parcel arrives and something is wrong, I can see at a glance when I placed the order and how long I have left to send it back. Most shops give you a few weeks, but the clock starts earlier than you think.

There is a quieter benefit too. Seeing every purchase in my own handwriting makes me pause before the next one. I still buy plenty of things I do not strictly need, but I buy them on purpose.

You do not need a special notebook. Mine is a cheap one from the corner shop, and it lives in the kitchen drawer next to the takeaway menus. The point is to make the record before you forget, not to make it pretty.

If you try this, give it a month before you decide whether it is worth it. I suspect you will find at least one thing that surprises you.
