Five small changes that made my morning coffee better

By Priya Nair · 6 min read

I have been making coffee at home for about ten years, and for most of that time it was fine. Not bad, not memorable. Then last winter I started changing one thing at a time, and by spring my morning cup was the best part of the day.

The first change was the simplest: I started weighing the beans. A cheap kitchen scale removed most of the guesswork. I settled on 15 grams of coffee for every 250 grams of water, and suddenly two cups in a row tasted the same.

The second was water temperature. I used to pour straight off the boil. Letting the kettle rest for thirty seconds took the harsh edge off lighter roasts without making darker ones taste flat.

Third, I bought beans in smaller bags, more often. Coffee loses a lot of its aroma in the weeks after roasting, and a bag that lasts ten days is better than one that lasts a month.

Fourth, grind size. I had been grinding too fine for my pour-over cone, which made the water drain slowly and the coffee taste bitter. A few clicks coarser fixed it.

Finally, I started paying attention. Writing down what I changed and how it tasted turned a chore into a hobby. None of this needs expensive equipment, just a little curiosity.
